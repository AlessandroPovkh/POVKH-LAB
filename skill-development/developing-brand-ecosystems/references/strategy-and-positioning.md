# Strategy and positioning

## Evidence before invention

Separate verified knowledge, stakeholder intent, observed behavior, hypotheses, and unknowns. Label hypotheses. Never turn a convenient assumption into public copy.

## Strategy model

Define only what the task needs:

- subject and offer;
- audience and context;
- job/problem and desired change;
- promise and proof;
- category and meaningful difference;
- values expressed as decisions;
- character and voice;
- naming architecture and product hierarchy.

For a consequential choice, provide 2–3 distinct options. Evaluate each against relevance, differentiation, credibility, extensibility, operational fit, and known constraints. Recommend one and name what evidence could change the recommendation.

## Deliverable

Create one normative strategy source with owner, scope, decision status, last review, evidence links, approved language, hypotheses, and excluded claims. Downstream copy and design must point back to it.

## Guardrails

- Preserve a strong existing position unless change is requested or evidence shows failure.
- Prefer specific tension and proof over generic aspiration.
- Keep internal language, public claims, and legal claims distinct.
- Define success signals without inventing baseline numbers or targets.
